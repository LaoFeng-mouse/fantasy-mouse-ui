# Product-style derivation

The presentation system is derived from the steering committee’s need to compare, sequence, challenge, and approve a fictional infrastructure portfolio. The Fantasy Mouse assets are character evidence only and provide no authority for layout, palette, typography, materials, or components.

| Target-product evidence | Derived presentation treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Executive steering committee; finite meeting time; consequential approval | Decision-first hierarchy, concise headlines, one argument per slide, and a visible approval ask. |
| Eight-step narrative from orientation to approval | Fixed sequence: title, decision, gap, priorities, portfolio, roadmap, risks, approval; no detours or appendix-dependent logic. |
| Illustrative scenario with invented figures | Persistent figure label `Illustrative planning scenario — not a forecast`; precise language that separates scenario assumptions from external facts. |
| Need to understand relationships and sequencing | Native charts and geometric diagrams only when they expose a gap, comparison, dependency, phase gate, or mitigation relationship. |
| Need for trust, scrutiny, and editability | Swiss/data-driven system with a disciplined grid, asymmetric hierarchy, generous negative space, native editable text/shapes, precise alignment, and restrained decoration. |
| Dense but presentation-scale information | At least 50 pt deck title, 35 pt slide titles, 24 pt subheads, and 16 pt body; short labels, direct annotation, and no UI-like card grids. |
| Three linked priorities | A restrained semantic accent system that consistently distinguishes visibility, flexibility, and resilience without implying measured performance. |
| Approval and risk accountability | Literal status language, explicit owners and phase gates, high-contrast risk/mitigation pairings, and no playful treatment of authoritative decisions. |
| Presenter mouse requested | Character occupies only supporting space, points to real evidence, and never becomes a logo, navigation device, decorative pattern, or source of claims. |

## Visual system

- **Surface:** editable 16:9 presentation at 1280 × 720 design coordinates.
- **Grid:** consistent page margins and a modular column/baseline system; controlled asymmetry should create hierarchy without sacrificing alignment.
- **Palette:** warm white or white ground, near-black type, cool neutral rules, and a limited set of accessible semantic accents. Reserve any warning color for risk or decision emphasis. Do not copy the palettes of the bundled compositions.
- **Typography:** neutral grotesk/sans-serif family with tabular numerals where available; sentence-case titles, strong weight contrast, tight display leading, and readable body leading.
- **Materials:** flat presentation surfaces and native vector geometry. Exclude paper texture, tape, sticker shadows, thick cartoon outlines, decorative window chrome, and ornamental dashboard treatments.
- **Borders and elevation:** fine rules or filled planes only where needed to clarify grouping; minimal radius; no gratuitous shadows.
- **Data graphics:** direct labels, visible units, restrained axes, and annotations adjacent to the evidence. Every quantitative object carries the illustrative/not-forecast label.
- **Motion:** none required. If transitions are later added, keep them brief, functional, and compatible with reduced-motion playback.
- **Sources:** add `[Sources]` notes for every generated or mouse asset and for any external item; fictional scenario numbers should be identified as such.

## Slide composition

1. **Title:** large typographic field with a compact modernization horizon cue; presenter is secondary.
2. **Decision:** dominant approval statement plus a concise definition of the staged portfolio and its gates.
3. **Gap:** one direct chart or slope comparison showing the illustrative demand/reliability gap.
4. **Priorities:** one linked three-part sequence, not three detached cards.
5. **Portfolio:** one comparison plane that makes trade-offs and the recommendation immediately legible.
6. **Roadmap:** one horizontal or stepped phase sequence with dependencies and decision gates.
7. **Risks:** one risk-to-mitigation structure with owners and gate implications.
8. **Approval:** one decisive ask with conditions, owners, and immediate next actions.

## Accessibility and responsive/editable behavior

- Maintain WCAG-aware contrast for text, data marks, and semantic accents; never rely on color alone.
- Preserve logical reading order, descriptive object names, and concise alt text for the presenter and meaningful graphics.
- Keep all essential labels at or above the specified type sizes and away from crop/overflow edges.
- Use redundant shape, label, and position cues for the three priorities, portfolio states, roadmap phases, and risks.
- Keep charts, text, and shapes editable and grouped by meaning; resizing must preserve margins, label association, and figure disclaimers.
- Do not claim accessibility, rendering, or visual acceptance until the real deck has been inspected in the target presentation tool.

## Intentionally excluded composition traits

The bundled character examples do not authorize their layout, palette, paper texture, taped labels, thick borders, title treatment, spacing, window chrome, or character placement. None of those traits belongs in this Swiss/data-driven committee deck unless independently justified by the presentation workflow; no such justification is present here.
